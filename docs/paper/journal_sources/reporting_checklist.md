Checklist of the *Guidelines for Developing and Reporting Machine Learning Predictive Models in Biomedical Research* (Luo W, Phung D, Tran T, et al. J Med Internet Res. 2016;18(12):e323), completed for this study. The study evaluates a diagnostic system built on a pretrained language model and fixed tools; no model was trained or fitted on the test data. Where an item concerns model fitting it is marked as not applicable, with the reason. Section names refer to the main manuscript; "Appendix 1" is Multimedia Appendix 1.

| Item | Topic | Checklist item (abridged) | Where reported / response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title: nature of study | Identify the report as introducing a predictive model | Title: an evaluation study of a diagnostic agent; the agent is introduced in Methods ("The Agent"). |
| 2 | Abstract: structured summary | Background, objectives, data sources, performance with point estimates and confidence intervals, conclusion | Abstract (Background, Objective, Methods, Results with 95% CIs for the primary comparisons, Conclusions). |
| 3 | Introduction: rationale | Clinical goal; current practice and accuracy of existing models | Introduction (paragraphs 1–4): phenotype-driven diagnosis of rare diseases; reported accuracies of tools and language-model agents and why they are hard to interpret. |
| 4 | Introduction: objectives | Nature of the study; target of prediction; benefit to the clinical goal | Introduction (last paragraph): ranking the confirmed diagnosis from a patient's findings; the three contributions. |
| 5 | Methods: setting | Clinical setting; context, size and duration of data | Methods, "Cases" and Table 1: published cases, patients shared by families and clinicians, diagnostic-laboratory and clinic cases, and case reports, with sizes and dates; Appendix 1, S1 and S3. |
| 6 | Methods: prediction problem | Measurement of the goal; retrospective or prospective; diagnostic or prognostic; form of the model; cost of errors; success criteria | Methods, "Task and Scoring": retrospective, diagnostic, a ranked list of up to ten diseases; solved at rank k; prespecified primary comparison and significance level ("Plans and Records"; Appendix 1, S7). The cost of errors is discussed under "Limitations" (no clinical use was tested). |
| 7 | Methods: data preparation | Sources and ethics; inclusion and exclusion; time span and size; units; predictors and prevention of leakage; preprocessing; missing values; basic statistics; validation strategy and metrics; derivation and validation split | Data sources and ethics: "Cases", "Ethical Considerations". Inclusion and exclusion, time spans, sizes: "Cases", Table 1, Appendix 1 S1 and S3. Unit: one case. Predictors: HPO terms and sex; findings recorded as absent withheld ("Task and Scoring"). Leakage prevention: masking of each case's source paper and an audit of every tool output ("Knowledge and Masking"). Missing answers count as misses. Basic statistics: number of findings and diseases per set ("Cases"; Appendix 1 S10). Validation: paper-level held-out split, a temporal set, external sets, and three prespecified tests on cases never used in development. Metrics: top-1/3/5/10 accuracy with Wilson 95% CIs; exact sign tests on paired cases; Holm adjustment for the primary tests. ROC curves and calibration plots do not apply to a ranked list of diseases. |
| 8 | Methods: model building | Variables; class balance; candidate techniques and justification; selection metric and strategy | Not applicable as model fitting: the agent is a pretrained language model with fixed prompts and tools; nothing was fitted to test data. Versions and the rule that chose between them on development data: "The Agent" and Appendix 1 S2. Use of a single inexpensive model is justified in the Introduction (what the tools add, not scale). |
| 9 | Results: final model and performance | Performance in the prespecified metrics with CIs; comparison with other models; interpretation; best- and worst-predicted subpopulations | Results; Tables 2–5; Figures 3–6. Comparison with published systems: held-out and frontier-model comparisons; DeepRare (Appendix 1 S8). Subpopulations: by number of findings, by CADA cohort, by kind of disease identifier (Tables 4–5; Appendix 1 S10). |
| 10 | Discussion: clinical implications | Implications of the performance | Discussion, "Principal Results" (cost per case, retrieval of the correct knowledge-base entry, evidence for each candidate); no clinical benefit is claimed. |
| 11 | Discussion: limitations | Input and output assumptions; pitfalls of interpretation; bias of the data; generalizability | Discussion, "Limitations". |
| 12 | Discussion: unexpected results | Unexpected findings | Results: the first version's loss to its own model on external patients and its cause; the identifier-versus-name difference on the case reports. |
