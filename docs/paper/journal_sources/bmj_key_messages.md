**What is already known on this topic** Language-model agents are reported to diagnose rare diseases from a patient's phenotype with high accuracy, on published cases that phenotype knowledge bases were curated from and that the models may have read.

**What this study adds** Masking each test case's source paper halved a published tool's first-place accuracy, and two revisions of a low-cost agent led their own model in three prespecified tests on cases never used in their development, although on case reports published after the model's cutoff much of the lead was retrieval of the correct knowledge-base entry rather than diagnosis.

**How this study might affect research, practice or policy** Evaluations of diagnostic decision support should mask case sources, test on patients outside the development source and fix the analysis plan before the run.
